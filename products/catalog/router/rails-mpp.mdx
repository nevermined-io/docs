The **Merchant Payment Protocol (MPP)** is the other pay-per-call protocol the Router speaks. Where x402 advertises its terms in an `accepts` array, an MPP merchant answers with an RFC 7235 challenge:

```
HTTP/1.1 402 Payment Required
WWW-Authenticate: Payment id="…", realm="service.example", method="tempo",
                  intent="charge", request="<base64url>", expires="…"
```

The Router decodes that challenge, signs the payment, and hands back an `Authorization: Payment <…>` credential. Your agent never loads an MPP SDK and never learns the envelope.

## What's supported

| | Supported |
| --- | --- |
| **Method** | `tempo` — a native Tempo transaction pulling a Tempo stablecoin |
| **Intent** | `charge` — a one-off payment for this request |
| **Networks** | Tempo mainnet (chain `4217`) and Tempo Moderato, the public testnet (chain `42431`) |
| **Assets** | Whatever the operator has allowlisted for that chain (see below) |

Subscriptions and metered streams aren't supported, and a challenge advertising one is refused rather than approximated. **Sessions** (payment channels) are being rolled out — see **Sessions** below.

<Note>
**You don't have to know a service speaks MPP.** In [mode B](/products/catalog/router/how-it-works) the Router detects the protocol from the 402 itself, and the detected protocol always wins over the optional `protocol` hint in your request. Send the same `/route` call you'd send for x402.
</Note>

## The asset allowlist

MPP challenges name their payment token by contract address, and the challenge comes from the merchant — an untrusted source. So the Router will only settle a token the operator has **explicitly allowlisted for that chain**, via `ROUTER_TEMPO_ASSETS_<chainId>`.

This is **fail-closed**: an unset or empty allowlist rejects *every* asset on that chain. A payment naming a token that isn't on the list is refused with **`400 BCK.ROUTER.0001`** before anything is signed.

If MPP payments fail with that code on a deployment where x402 works fine, an unconfigured allowlist is the first thing to check — the two rails are configured independently.

<Warning>
  **A service appearing in the Agent Services Catalog does not mean your deployment can pay it.**
  The catalog describes services; the allowlist is a property of the deployment. Where the MPP rail
  is not enabled, *every* MPP service is refused with `400 BCK.ROUTER.0001 … not allowlisted` while
  x402 services pay normally — which reads like a broken merchant and is not one.

  Retrying will not help, and neither will trying a different MPP service: they all fail the same
  way. Check the allowlist for the chain, or ask whoever operates your deployment.
</Warning>

| Chain | Chain id | Ledger network |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo mainnet | `4217` | `tempo` |
| Tempo Moderato (public testnet) | `42431` | `tempo-moderato` |

<Warning>
The stablecoin contract address is **not the same on both chains**. Allowlisting a testnet address on mainnet, or vice versa, produces a `400` that looks like the rail is broken.
</Warning>

The two chains settle in genuinely different tokens — the mainnet address is not derivable from the testnet one:

| Chain | Token | Address | Decimals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo mainnet (`4217`) | `USDC.e` — Bridged USDC (Stargate) | `0x20c000000000000000000000b9537d11c60e8b50` | 6 |
| Tempo Moderato (`42431`) | PathUSD — a Tempo system predeploy | `0x20c0000000000000000000000000000000000000` | 6 |

Both begin `0x20c0` and differ only in the tail, so they are easy to misread. Confirm against a real challenge from the service you intend to pay before sending funds anywhere.

## Funding your wallet

Like x402, MPP `charge` is a **pull**: the merchant takes the amount from your own wallet on the Tempo chain in question. Fund the address on your Delegation (`providerPaymentMethodId`) with the token for that chain before you call — and fund for the merchant's amount **plus** the [routing fee](/products/catalog/router/how-it-works#fund-for-the-amount-plus-the-fee). On this rail the fee is a **second on-chain transfer** the Router broadcasts from the same wallet, so the merchant's price alone doesn't cover both.

An underfunded wallet fails with **`402 BCK.ROUTER.0009`**, checked *before* signing — the balance has to clear the amount **and** the fee, so a wallet holding only the quoted price is refused here. Nothing is minted and no budget is reserved. That pre-check is specific to this chain-funded rail; [card-funded MPP](#card-funded-mpp) reaches the same error by a different route.

On **Moderato** you can fund yourself in one RPC call — no faucet UI, no wallet, no signing:

```bash
curl -sX POST https://rpc.moderato.tempo.xyz \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"tempo_fundAddress","params":["0xYOUR_WALLET"]}'
```

It returns transaction hashes and lands test tokens. The balance needs a block to appear, so a `balanceOf` read immediately afterwards can still show zero — wait a few seconds and read again before concluding it did nothing.

On **mainnet there is no faucet**: you have to bridge or transfer real USDC.e in yourself.

## What the Router refuses

The MPP handler validates the decoded challenge before it signs, and fails closed on anything it can't fully account for:

- **Wrong method or intent** — anything other than `tempo` / `charge`.
- **Unsupported chain** — a chain id outside the table above.
- **Malformed recipient, currency, or amount** — addresses must be valid hex; the amount must be a positive integer.
- **A non-allowlisted asset** — see above.
- **Multi-recipient payouts.** A challenge that carries `splits` is refused outright.

<Warning>
**Why splits are refused.** An MPP `charge` can name a primary recipient *and* a list of additional payout recipients. Only the primary one is ever validated against your Delegation, so honouring splits would move real funds to addresses nobody checked. The Router rejects any split-bearing challenge outright — unconditionally, whether or not your Delegation restricts recipients — rather than paying the part it can vouch for.
</Warning>

After signing, the Router decodes the credential it actually produced and compares it against the challenge it validated. If they diverge, the credential is discarded and never leaves the process — so a merchant can't get one thing approved and a different thing signed.

## Receipts and settlement

On a paid response an MPP merchant returns a **`Payment-Receipt`** header. In mode B the Router decodes it and stores its `reference` as the record's `txHash` — for `tempo`, the on-chain settlement transaction hash.

- Receipt present and valid → record `Settled`.
- Receipt missing, oversized, or malformed → record stays **`Issued`**. The hop succeeded and you got your resource; only the settlement anchor is pending. This is a normal state, not a failure, and the Router will not fail an already-paid call over a bad receipt.
- Merchant returns another 402 to the paid hop (it rejected the credential) → record `Failed`.

<Note>
In **mode A** the Router isn't in the request path, so it never sees the receipt. Closing the record means decoding the receipt yourself and reporting the reference — which needs the MPP codec. This is the main reason to prefer mode B on this rail.
</Note>

The reference is merchant-controlled and stored **unverified** — see [how it works](/products/catalog/router/how-it-works#how-settlement-gets-recorded).

## Card-funded MPP

Some MPP merchants advertise `method="stripe"` instead — a card-funded rail where the Router mints a Shared Payment Token from your enrolled card rather than pulling a stablecoin. The API surface is identical: same endpoints, same Delegation model, same ledger.

The differences that matter:

- It's funded by a **card** Delegation, not a crypto one. A crypto Delegation fails on this rail and vice versa.
- There's **no recipient allowlist** — a card Delegation is deliberately not bound to a seller, so the guardrails are the spending cap and the expiry.
- Settlement is a payment-processor reference rather than a transaction hash, and it isn't known until the merchant redeems — so the record stays `Issued` pending reconciliation.
- **There's no balance to pre-check, so there isn't one.** Where the chain-funded rail reads your balance before signing, this one simply attempts the card. A decline surfaces as the same **`402 BCK.ROUTER.0009`**, and nothing is minted and no budget is reserved — but you've had a real decline on your card, not a check that spared you one.

<Warning>
This rail is **feature-flagged off by default and sandbox-only** while the underlying token issuance is still in limited availability. With the flag off, a card-funded payment is refused with `400 BCK.ROUTER.0001` before anything is minted. Don't build against it in production yet.
</Warning>

## Sessions (payment channels)

Some MPP merchants sell only over **`intent="session"`**: a one-way payment channel instead of a payment per call. Your wallet locks a **deposit** in an on-chain escrow at the first call, and every call after that carries a signed, **cumulative** voucher rather than a transaction. The merchant can take at most the highest voucher it holds, never more than the deposit, and whatever it did not take comes back to your wallet when the channel closes.

<Warning>
Session support is **switched off on Nevermined's deployments** until its rollout completes. While it is off, a service that offers only a session is refused with `400 BCK.ROUTER.0001`, exactly as before. The behaviour below is what you get once it is on.
</Warning>

How the Router pays a session:

- **Mode B only.** `POST /router/route`, `/router/svc/<slug>` and `/router/proxy` pay a session; `POST /router/payments` refuses one with **`400 BCK.ROUTER.0041`**, because the Router has to read the merchant's receipt after every call to keep the channel consistent, and in mode A it never sees it.
- **Charge first.** A service that offers both a `charge` and a `session` is paid over `charge`, whatever order it lists them in.
- **One channel per merchant.** The Router keeps one channel per wallet, merchant and token, and reuses it for every call. Concurrent calls to the same merchant are signed one after the other on that channel.
- **The deposit** is the smallest of the merchant's suggested deposit, **$10** by default, your Delegation's remaining budget less what your wallet already holds unspent in its other open channels (below), and your wallet balance minus a little for gas. If even one call cannot fit, the call is refused before anything is signed: `402 BCK.ROUTER.0009` for the wallet, `402 BCK.ROUTER.0003` for the budget.
- **Your budget moves by the price of each call, never by the deposit.** The deposit is locked, not spent: it leaves your wallet balance, but it is not charged to your Delegation, and it comes back at close. What it does do is hold room: the unspent deposit in your other open channels is subtracted from what a new channel, or a top-up, may lock. So `402 BCK.ROUTER.0003` can answer a first call to a new merchant while `GET /delegation/{id}` still shows budget left; that room comes back as those channels close.
- **When the deposit is used up**, the Router **tops the channel up**: it sends the merchant a signed top-up of the deposit, the way the MPP client does, and pays the call once the merchant confirms it. The channel's deposit never grows past **$10** in total by default. A top-up your Delegation or wallet cannot cover is refused like an opening deposit, `402 BCK.ROUTER.0003` or `402 BCK.ROUTER.0009`, and the channel stays open. A channel already at the $10 limit, a merchant that refuses or does not confirm the top-up (or refuses the call right after it), or a merchant that takes no top-up at all moves the channel to **closing**, and the call is refused with **`409 BCK.ROUTER.0042`** (retryable). Retry later with the same `requestId`: the next call after the close opens a new channel, and the close refunds the unspent deposit to your wallet.
- **Busy, not refused.** When another call on the same channel holds it too long, your wallet's signer or balance read does not answer in time, or a top-up of the channel is still in flight or the service could not be reached to take one, the call is refused with **`503 BCK.ROUTER.0043`** (retryable, `params.stage` says which) before anything is signed. Retry after a few seconds with the same `requestId`.
- **A channel the merchant cannot use is closed, not retried forever.** If the merchant refuses the call that opened the channel, never acknowledges the channel, or the opening call never reached it, the channel moves to closing, and the next calls answer `0042` until it has closed.
- **The routing fee is collected later, never per call.** A session call is charged the same [routing fee](/products/catalog/router/how-it-works#fund-for-the-amount-plus-the-fee) as any other, on its price (never on the deposit), and your budget reserves it with the call. It is not a transfer of its own: the Router adds up your session fees and collects them in one transfer from your wallet, with your other Tempo routing fees, once your calls were delivered. A call the merchant did not deliver is not charged it. Where that collection cannot happen — the payment is funded by an organization wallet, or the deployment does not collect session fees on that network — the call is refused with **`422 BCK.ROUTER.0044`** (`params.cause`: `org-wallet` or `network`) before anything is signed. From an organization wallet, pay from a delegation funded by your own wallet instead.
- **Settled per channel, when it closes.** A session call is no transaction of its own (the receipt's reference is the channel), so its payment record reads `Issued`, `deliveryStatus: "pending"`, until its channel closes and the Router has reconciled it: at best 15 minutes after the channel's last call plus up to 10 minutes, longer for a channel kept busy or one the merchant will not close (the escrow's grace period applies). The money moves once, at the close, and the Router then compares what the escrow paid the merchant with the channel's calls, crediting delivered calls first:
  - a delivered call the merchant was paid for becomes `Settled`, its `txHash` the channel's close transaction;
  - a call the merchant was not paid for gives its price back to your Delegation budget, and reads `Settled` if it was delivered anyway or `Failed` if not;
  - a call the merchant was paid for without delivering it stays charged: `deliveryStatus` `charged_not_delivered` after a refusal, `charged_unconfirmed` after an error answer. That is the merchant taking money it should not have, and the reconciliation flags each such call to Nevermined;
  - a call the merchant charged only part of releases the rest of its price.

  You cannot report a session payment settled yourself: `POST /router/payments/{id}/settled` answers `409 BCK.ROUTER.0005`, because the channel's close is its settlement.

The same checks as on a charge apply before anything is signed — method, chain, recipient, the asset allowlist, your Delegation's recipient scope — plus a few of the channel's own: the escrow must be Tempo's channel escrow, gas must be paid in the channel's token, and the service must bill per request.

## Operator configuration

| Env var | Required | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ROUTER_TEMPO_ASSETS_<chainId>` | **Yes** | Comma-separated allowlist of payment-token addresses settleable on that chain. **Fail-closed** — unset rejects everything. |
| `ROUTER_TEMPO_RPC_URL_<chainId>` | No | Override RPC endpoint for that chain. Defaults to the chain's public RPC. |
| `ROUTER_STRIPE_SPT_ENABLED` | No | Master switch for the card-funded rail. Fail-closed; sandbox only. |
| `ROUTER_MPP_SESSION_ENABLED` | No | Master switch for paying session merchants. Default `false`: a session is refused as if unsupported. |
| `ROUTER_MPP_SESSION_MAX_DEPOSIT` | No | The most one channel may hold in escrow, in US dollars of its stablecoin. Default `10`. |
